Methods for Retirement Savings Goals & the 4% Rule

Secure your future. Find out how the 4% withdrawal rate works, how inflation cuts cash holdings, and standard pension compounding calculators.

Written by the CalcUni Editorial Team | Updated: May 2026

Planning for retirement is one of the most important long-term financial tasks you will face. With rising life expectancies and the decline of traditional company pensions, the responsibility of building a secure retirement nest egg falls entirely on individual workers.

1. The 4% Rule Explained

Developed during the Trinity Study in 1998, the 4% Safe Withdrawal Rate is a guideline designed to prevent retirees from running out of money over a 30-year retirement. It suggests:

  1. Withdraw exactly 4% of your total retirement portfolio in the first year of retirement.
  2. In subsequent years, adjust the withdrawal amount to account for annual inflation.

For example, if you retire with a $1,000,000 portfolio, you can safely withdraw $40,000 in Year 1. If inflation is 3%, you withdraw $41,200 in Year 2, and so on.

2. Calculating Your Target Nest Egg

To find the total savings required to retire using the 4% rule, you can multiply your desired annual retirement income by 25:

Retirement Target = Desired Annual Income × 25

If you want a comfortable retirement income of $60,000 per year, you need a target savings portfolio of $60,000 × 25 = $1,500,000.

3. The Magic of Compounding Interest

The earlier you start saving, the less money you have to personally invest, thanks to compound interest. If you start saving $500/month at age 25, you will accumulate over $1.1 million by age 65 (assuming a 7% average annual return). If you wait until age 35 to start, you will accumulate less than half that amount!
