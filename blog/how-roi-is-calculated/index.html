How to Calculate Return on Investment (ROI) & Asset Yields

Unlock commercial growth. Learn the basic ROI calculation, compound annualized return structures, and how to measure trade profitability.

Written by the CalcUni Editorial Team | Updated: May 2026

Whether you are investing in real estate, stock portfolios, marketing campaigns, or corporate machinery, evaluating financial performance is key. The most universal metric for this is Return on Investment (ROI), which measures the efficiency and profitability of a commercial asset or trade.

1. The Standard ROI Formula

ROI is represented as a percentage. It divides the net profit of an investment by its initial cost:

ROI (%) = [ (Current Value of Investment - Initial Cost) / Initial Cost ] × 100

2. Annualized ROI: Comparing Across Time

Simple ROI fails to account for the time factor. A 50% return is incredible if achieved in 6 months, but poor if it takes 15 years. To compare assets fairly, you should use the Annualized ROI formula:

Annualized ROI (%) = [ (1 + Simple ROI)^(1 / Years) - 1 ] × 100

3. ROI vs. Profit Margin

While they sound similar, ROI and profit margins measure different things:

  • ROI: Focuses on the return generated by a specific cash outlay (e.g., spending $10k on ads and generating $15k in sales is a 50% ROI).
  • Profit Margin: Focuses on the efficiency of sales (e.g., selling a product for $100 that costs $60 to produce represents a 40% gross profit margin).
