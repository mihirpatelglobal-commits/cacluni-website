The Car Loan Calculator Formula

The calculation relies on the following standard formula:

M = P * [ r(1 + r)^n ] / [ (1 + r)^n - 1 ]

Where: * M = monthly auto payment * P = principal loan amount (purchase price minus down payment/trade-in value) * r = monthly interest rate (annual interest rate divided by 12) * n = total number of monthly payments (term length in months) Explanation: This formula calculates the fixed monthly payment required to amortize an auto loan over a specified term (commonly 36 to 84 months).

Step-by-Step Worked Example

Example Calculation

Inputs: * Vehicle Price = $25,000 * Down Payment = $3,000 * Trade-in Value = $2,000 * Interest Rate = 4% per annum * Loan Term = 5 years (60 months) Calculation: * Step 1: Calculate Loan Principal (P) = $25,000 - $3,000 - $2,000 = $20,000 * Step 2: Compute monthly interest rate (r) = 0.04 / 12 = 0.003333 * Step 3: Apply formula: M = 20,000 * [ 0.003333(1.003333)^60 ] / [ (1.003333)^60 - 1 ] Result: * Monthly Payment = $368.33 What This Means: You will pay $368.33 per month for 60 months to fully purchase the car, incurring a total interest expense of $2,099.73.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

❓ Should I choose a longer car loan term to lower my payments?

Choosing a longer loan term (e.g., 72 or 84 months) lowers your monthly payment, but it significantly increases the total interest you pay over the life of the loan. It also increases the risk of being "upside down" on your loan, where you owe more than the car is worth.

❓ How does a trade-in value affect my car loan?

A trade-in value acts as a down payment. It reduces the total principal amount you need to borrow for your new vehicle, which directly lowers your monthly payment and reduces the total interest you will pay over the term.

❓ What is a pre-computed interest auto loan?

A pre-computed interest auto loan calculates the total interest due at the start of the loan and adds it to the principal. Paying off this type of loan early does not save you as much interest as a simple interest loan.

❓ What is GAP insurance and do I need it?

GAP insurance covers the difference between what your car is worth and what you owe on your loan if the vehicle is totaled. It is recommended if you have a low down payment or a long loan term.

❓ How does my credit score affect my car loan interest rate?

Lenders use your credit score to assess risk. A higher credit score qualifies you for lower interest rates, which makes borrowing cheaper, while a lower credit score results in higher interest rates and monthly payments.
